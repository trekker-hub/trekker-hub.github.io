Overview

What this project is

This project is an FPGA-based onboard computer designed for 1U/3U CubeSat missions operating in low Earth orbit. The platform handles telemetry processing, attitude control data, and payload data compression in real time, all within the severe power and size constraints of a small satellite.

The central design challenge was correctness guarantees under radiation-induced bit flips. Space hardware can't be patched from the ground mid-mission, so every critical function was developed using ADA-SPARK — a formally verifiable subset of Ada — to generate mathematical proofs that the logic behaves exactly as specified under all possible inputs.

The FPGA fabric was designed in VHDL with triple-modular redundancy on safety-critical voter logic, and the system was validated against single-event upset injection to confirm fault tolerance.

Reflection

What I learned

Formal verification changes how you design

Writing ADA-SPARK forces you to state your assumptions explicitly as preconditions before you write a single line of logic. That discipline — thinking about invariants first — made the entire design cleaner, not just more provable.

Space tolerances are genuinely different

Radiation-induced single event upsets are probabilistic, not deterministic. Designing for faults you can't predict or prevent but can detect and correct via TMR gave me a new mental model for reliability engineering.
